About Me

Builder at the intersection of architecture and strategy

I've spent 18 years at the intersection of enterprise architecture and business growth — first as the person in the trenches solving the hard integration problems, then as the person helping organizations see what becomes possible when those problems are solved well.

My career has been shaped by a straightforward observation: most enterprise AI initiatives don't fail because of the AI. They fail because the underlying data and integration infrastructure isn't ready for it. That gap is where I work. At Salesforce, I lead enterprise engagements that connect technical architecture decisions to platform growth — turning integration audits into adoption roadmaps and driving 19% YoY expansion across our enterprise base.

I'm equally comfortable presenting a reference architecture to a CTO and digging into a connector configuration with a solutions architect. I've built and scaled global teams, run pre-GA pilots with enterprise customers, deployed AI automation into real support operations, and carried the Voice of Customer back to Product Engineering in a way that actually shapes the roadmap.

Outside the day job, I speak at industry events, contribute to the MuleSoft community, and hold an AI Business Strategies certificate from UC Berkeley Haas. I'm always interested in conversations at the edge of enterprise integration, AI infrastructure, and what the next generation of autonomous enterprise systems will look like.

How I drive results

Architecture reviews that turn into revenue

I turn integration gap analyses into adoption roadmaps — identifying exactly where customers are leaving platform value on the table, including where SI implementations are patching over native capabilities. That work drove 19% YoY consumption growth across our enterprise base.

I don't just recommend AI — I've deployed it

When we built our internal AI support automation platform, I led adoption from day one: writing training utterances, running tuning cycles, measuring deflection rates, and iterating until the model reliably handled what we needed. The result was a meaningful reduction in manual escalation work and a team that had actual confidence in the tool.

Field signals that actually reach the roadmap

Enterprise customers tell me things they don't put in support tickets. I built structured programs to capture those signals — recurring architectural pain points, integration patterns that keep breaking, feature gaps that block adoption — and deliver them to Product Engineering as curated, evidence-backed roadmap input, not anecdotes.

The advisor in the room when it matters

I sit in rooms where integration decisions worth eight figures get made. CIOs and enterprise architects come to me for a straight read on what architecture actually makes sense for where they're headed — not a sales pitch. My job is to help them build the foundation that doesn't have to be rebuilt in three years.

Leading global teams through real complexity

I've run distributed teams across time zones with competing priorities, managed through reorgs, and led the kind of cross-functional work where everyone owns part of the outcome but nobody owns the whole thing. The lesson: clarity on what's actually blocking progress — not status meetings — is what moves organizations forward.
